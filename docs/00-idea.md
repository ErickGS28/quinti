# 0. The idea, explained simply

## In one sentence

**Quintti is a community fund that automatically pays digital dollars to families in a zone hit by a natural disaster, within minutes and with no paperwork, using a contract on Stellar.**

The family abroad can pay for it too: someone living in another country protects their loved ones with a small premium.

## The name

*Qínti* (or *kinti*) means **hummingbird** in Quechua. Quintti takes its name from it. The hummingbird is small, fast and arrives exactly where it is needed. That is the idea: help that is small, quick and direct.

## The problem we solve

When an earthquake, flood or hurricane hits a zone:

1. **The money is slow.** Traditional insurance requires forms, assessments and waits of weeks. Just when a family most needs liquidity, it has none.
2. **The most vulnerable are left out.** People with low incomes rarely have insurance: it is expensive, requires paperwork and was not designed for them.
3. **The money gets trapped.** If the town bank goes down or there are no ATMs, money that reaches a local account cannot be used.
4. **Family abroad does not know how to help.** Those who live elsewhere want to protect their loved ones, but can only send a remittance *after* the disaster, when the need has already become urgent.
5. **Aid is not traceable.** Donations are often opaque: it is unclear how much arrived or to whom.

## How it works (3 steps)

1. **You protect.** A person (for example, a son or daughter abroad) enrolls a relative in their zone, chooses a coverage amount and pays a small monthly premium in digital dollars. They can also name who will receive the money (the relative, or another family member outside the zone).
2. **The disaster is confirmed.** Public data sources (for example, the USGS earthquake service) report an event that exceeds a threshold in a zone. A group of attesters confirms it on the network.
3. **The contract pays.** With no forms or assessments, the contract sends the money to the people enrolled in that zone. The rules are public and nobody can change them afterwards.

## An example

Luz lives in Quibdó. Her son Andrés works in another country and pays 2 USDC a month for her protection. A strong earthquake hits the region. Public data confirms the magnitude and the zone. Within minutes, the contract sends the agreed payout to Luz's wallet (or to her sister's in another city, if Luz set it up that way). She did not fill out any form or wait on anyone.

*(Fictional characters. Amounts are illustrative.)*

## Goal

- **Of the product:** a parametric fund that works end to end: enroll, contribute, confirm a real event and receive the payout.
- **Long term:** make protecting a family against a disaster cost about as much as a coffee a month, and make the money arrive when it is needed.

## Who it is for

| Who | What they get |
|-----|---------------|
| **Family in a risk zone** | Liquidity within minutes after the event, with no paperwork |
| **Person abroad** | A concrete, affordable way to protect loved ones *before* something happens |
| **Donor or organization** | Contribute to a fund whose rules and payouts are public and verifiable |

## What it is NOT

- **It is not regulated insurance.** It is a community aid fund; the legal framework is a step after the pilot.
- **It does not replace emergency humanitarian aid.** It provides liquidity for the first weeks and for recovery, not food or water in the first hours.
- **It does not cover individual losses.** It pays when a measurable condition is met (for example, the magnitude of an earthquake in a zone), not according to the damage to each house.
- **It is not a product ready for real money.** It is a testnet prototype.

## Why Stellar

- Cheap, fast payments, suited to very small premiums.
- The money lives on the ledger, not in a local bank that can go down.
- Rules and payouts in a public, auditable contract.
- Digital dollars with on-ramps and off-ramps (anchors) that connect to local currency.
